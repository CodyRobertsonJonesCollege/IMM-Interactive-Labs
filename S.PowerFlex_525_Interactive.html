01 / Fundamentals

What does a VFD do?

A variable frequency drive converts fixed-frequency AC input into a controlled-frequency, controlled-voltage output for an AC motor. Its major stages are a rectifier, DC bus, and inverter.

Key concepts

  1. Frequency (Hz) primarily determines synchronous motor speed.
  2. Voltage-to-frequency ratio helps maintain motor magnetic flux in basic V/Hz control.
  3. Acceleration/deceleration ramps control how quickly the commanded frequency changes.
  4. Current and thermal protection help protect the motor and drive.

Instructional explanation

For an induction motor, synchronous speed is:

Nₛ = 120f / P

Where f is frequency in Hz and P is the number of motor poles.

A 4-pole motor at 60 Hz has a synchronous speed of 1800 RPM. Its loaded rotor normally turns slightly slower because an induction motor requires slip to produce torque.

Increasing frequency beyond the motor's rated base frequency can move operation into a field-weakening region. Do not assume the motor can safely deliver rated torque or operate at arbitrary overspeed.

Why V/Hz matters

Below base frequency, a simple constant V/Hz strategy raises voltage approximately in proportion to frequency, helping maintain motor flux.

Above base frequency, available voltage is limited and the voltage-to-frequency ratio falls. This is the field-weakening region.

Real drives may add low-speed voltage boost, compensation and more sophisticated control algorithms. This plot is an idealized linear V/Hz teaching model, not an exact PF525 output prediction.

05 / Motor protection

FLA, service factor and overload current

P034 [Motor NP FLA] represents the motor nameplate full-load current. P033 [Motor OL Current] sets the motor overload current used by the drive's overload protection model.

Motor NP FLA · P034A

Service factor

Understanding service factor

A nameplate SF of 1.15 means that, under conditions allowed by the motor manufacturer, the motor may carry up to 115% of its rated load. It is not permission to continuously overload every motor.

Classroom calculation: Motor FLA × SF = candidate overload-current setting. For example, 2.0 A × 1.15 = 2.30 A.

Important: This is a teaching calculation, not an automatic programming recommendation. Check motor instructions, cooling, drive current rating, application duty, applicable codes and Rockwell documentation. P033 is not simply interchangeable with P034.

06 / EtherNet/IP configuration

Configure a simulated static IP

The PowerFlex 525 embedded EtherNet/IP interface can be addressed using parameter values. These controls produce a parameter worksheet only; they do not configure a drive.

Lab network topology

With a direct PC-to-drive Ethernet connection, the PC and drive generally need compatible IP/subnet settings. A gateway is usually unnecessary when communication remains on the local subnet.

Rockwell documented values: C128 = 1 selects parameters; C128 = 2 selects BOOTP (default). C129–C132 set IP octets, C133–C136 subnet octets, and C137–C140 gateway octets. A drive reset or power cycle is required for address changes to take effect.
